# RA-MoE-4E

### Regime-Aware Mixture-of-Experts for Robust Option Pricing

RA-MoE-4E is a theory-guided Mixture-of-Experts framework that dynamically combines a Black–Scholes–Merton (BSM) theoretical anchor with specialized neural experts for robust option pricing across heterogeneous market conditions.

The project investigates a broader machine-learning question:

> **Can theory-driven and data-driven experts specialize and cooperate through state-dependent dynamic routing?**

The framework was developed as my undergraduate thesis in Financial Mathematics and evaluated on S&P 500 (SPX) index options and Apple (AAPL) equity options.

---

## Overview

Classical option-pricing models provide strong theoretical structure and economic interpretability, but their assumptions may become restrictive under heterogeneous or rapidly changing market conditions.

Purely data-driven models offer greater flexibility but may sacrifice structural consistency and robustness.

RA-MoE-4E explores a hybrid approach by combining four heterogeneous experts through a regime-aware gating network:

1. **BSM Expert** — provides a theory-driven pricing anchor.
2. **Residual MLP Expert** — learns systematic deviations from the theoretical baseline.
3. **Deep MLP Expert** — captures nonlinear cross-sectional pricing relationships.
4. **Transformer Expert** — models temporal market dynamics.

A gating network dynamically assigns state-dependent weights to these experts, allowing the model to adapt its internal pricing mechanism across different market conditions.

---

## Architecture

RA-MoE-4E combines four heterogeneous experts through a **regime-aware gating network**.  
For each option observation, the final prediction is a dynamically weighted combination of the four expert outputs:

$$
P_{\mathrm{hybrid}} = \sum_{i=1}^{4} w_i E_i(X_i)
$$

where the gating weights satisfy

$$
w_i \geq 0, \qquad \sum_{i=1}^{4} w_i = 1.
$$

The weights are generated by a **state-dependent gating network** using market-regime and option-specific features. This allows the model to adaptively shift predictive responsibility among experts as market conditions change.

### Four Specialized Experts

| Expert | Role | Main Idea |
|---|---|---|
| **BSM** | Theoretical anchor | Provides a classical theory-based pricing benchmark |
| **Residual MLP** | Theory correction | Learns systematic deviations from the BSM baseline |
| **Deep MLP** | Cross-sectional learning | Captures nonlinear relationships across static market and option features |
| **Transformer** | Temporal learning | Models time-dependent market dynamics and sequential patterns |

The resulting architecture can be summarized as:

**Market & Option Features → Specialized Experts + Regime-Aware Gate → Dynamic Expert Weights → Final Prediction**

## Research Questions

This project investigates four main questions:

**RQ1.** Can dynamic expert routing improve pricing robustness across heterogeneous market regimes?

**RQ2.** Can theory-driven and neural experts provide complementary inductive biases?

**RQ3.** Do different experts specialize under different market structures?

**RQ4.** Can improved predictive performance be achieved while retaining economically meaningful pricing behaviour?

## Data

The empirical study uses two option markets with substantially different characteristics:

- **SPX index options:** 3,936,365 observations after filtering.
- **AAPL equity options:** 849,023 observations after filtering.

The analysis covers option characteristics, volatility measures, liquidity information, and temporal market features.

Raw option data are obtained from **OptionMetrics IvyDB U.S.**

> **Data availability:** Raw OptionMetrics data are not distributed in this repository because of licensing restrictions. The repository will provide preprocessing logic, expected input schemas, and reproducibility instructions where licensing permits.

## Training Strategy

Training follows a multi-stage procedure:

1. **Expert pretraining**  
   Specialized neural experts first learn their respective pricing functions.

2. **Gating-network learning**  
   The routing mechanism learns how to allocate weights across heterogeneous experts.

3. **Joint fine-tuning**  
   Experts and the gating mechanism are optimized jointly to improve overall performance.

The training objective combines predictive accuracy with regularization designed to control expert allocation and encourage structurally meaningful predictions.

## Main Results

The thesis evaluation found that RA-MoE-4E outperformed the BSM benchmark in both markets:

| Market | RA-MoE-4E RMSE | BSM RMSE | Reported Improvement |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| SPX | 1.297 | 1.379 | 11.55% |
| AAPL | 0.375 | 0.405 | 14.46% |

The larger improvement observed for AAPL suggests that adaptive expert combinations may become particularly useful when the assumptions underlying the theoretical baseline are less representative of the observed market.

Beyond aggregate pricing accuracy, the project evaluates:

- regime robustness;
- cross-sectional performance across moneyness and maturity;
- cross-market expert specialization;
- gating-weight interpretability;
- financial consistency;
- component ablation.

Detailed figures and evaluation outputs will be added as the repository is refactored for reproducibility.

## Model Interpretability

An important objective of RA-MoE-4E is to examine **expert specialization**, rather than treating the ensemble as a black box.

The gating weights provide a mechanism for studying how the model reallocates predictive responsibility among theoretical, residual, nonlinear, and temporal experts under different market conditions.

The empirical analysis compares gating behaviour across:

- SPX and AAPL;
- volatility regimes;
- moneyness regions;
- maturity groups.

This provides a way to investigate whether heterogeneous experts learn distinct functional roles.

---

## Ablation Study

Component-level ablation experiments evaluate the contribution of individual experts by removing them from the full architecture.

The analysis examines variants including:

- BSM baseline;
- RA-MoE-4E without the Residual MLP;
- RA-MoE-4E without the Deep MLP;
- RA-MoE-4E without the Transformer.

These experiments are used to investigate whether improvements arise from complementary expert specialization rather than model complexity alone.
